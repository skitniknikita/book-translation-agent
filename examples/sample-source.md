# Attention and Care

An original synthetic essay for testing Book Translation Agent.

## I. Giving time

Attention and care are related, but they are not interchangeable. We may attend
closely to a passing sound without accepting any responsibility for its source.
To care for a shared space, however, may require returning to it after the first
moment of interest has passed. This does not mean that attention is always brief
or that care is always deliberate.

Consider a reading group that meets in a public room. One participant notices a
broken lamp; another remembers it a week later and brings a replacement bulb.
Neither act by itself tells us everything about the participant's intentions.
The difference concerns the relation between an act and its continuation.[^1]

> A promise to return is not yet a return.

This sentence belongs to the present example. It is not a quotation from another
author and should not acquire a fictitious bibliographic reference in translation.

## II. Keeping a distinction

Three questions can guide the group without resolving the issue in advance:

- What drew our attention?
- What responsibility, if any, did we accept?
- What would count as continuing that responsibility?

The questions leave room for disagreement. A person may refuse a particular task
without being indifferent to the room, just as accepting a task does not guarantee
that it will be completed. An account of care should preserve both possibilities.

[^1]: Here, “continuation” refers to a relation across occasions, not necessarily
    to uninterrupted activity.
